## The Concept

A single web app bringing together goal tracking, financial management, health logging, and family tree mapping. Rather than just starting to build, I treated this as a real product design exercise.

## What I Did

**Market research.** Mapped the existing landscape — Notion, Obsidian, Monarch Money, MyHeritage — to understand what was already solved and where genuine gaps existed.

**Scope analysis.** Rigorous breakdown of which features were genuinely useful, which were scope creep, and what a realistic MVP looked like for a solo developer building in evenings and weekends.

**Feasibility assessment.** Evaluated architecture options, data modeling requirements, and realistic timeline estimates. Full vision: 18–24 months. Meaningful MVP: 3–4 months.

**Decision.** Chose not to build it — the market was saturated for most features, and the remaining unique value was too niche for the engineering investment at this stage. The exercise was its own output: a clear product thinking framework I now apply to every new idea.

## What I Took Away

- Scope risk for solo developers is the primary failure mode — not technical ability
- Market validation before code is always the right order
- A well-documented "decided not to build" is itself a valuable product artefact
- Applied this framework directly to evaluating automation ideas at D&H Sécheron
